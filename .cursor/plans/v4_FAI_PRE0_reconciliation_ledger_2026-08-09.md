## Disposition vocabulary — fixed in advance
`adopted` · `adopted_narrowed` · `already_present` *(name the OMNI carrier)* · `rejected_with_reason` · `out_of_scope` · `open`

**R1 adds three compound forms of `already_present`, because R0's flat form hid the arc's most important result.** When R0 could not say *"OMNI already has this, and here is the delta"*, it defaulted to `adopted` and **re-minted patterns the estate already owned**:
- **`already_present_with_extension`** — the carrier holds the pattern; a named clause is genuinely missing *(AB-01)*
- **`already_present_with_scope_extension`** — the carrier holds the **law**; it must reach a **new class of object** *(AB-05)*
- **`already_present_with_correction`** — the carrier is **more precise than the finding**, and the finding would have degraded it *(INV-08)*

**An `owner` must be an accountable role or seat.** A gate, a lane, a registry or a target artifact is a **destination**, never an owner. R0 violated this on seven rows; R1 repairs all seven and names the destination separately.

**Two dispositions have hard requirements, because both are how a ledger becomes a graveyard:**
- **`open`** is valid ONLY with **owner + trigger + target gate + blocking/non-blocking status.** "Open with an owner" alone is a parking lot.
- **`out_of_scope`** requires **an actual destination artifact** — not "later" and not "another arc."

**Every row carries four fields beyond its disposition:** `source_ref` (file + section) · `blocking_scope` (blocks G0 / blocks G1 / blocks G3 / blocks G4 / non-blocking) · `owner_or_destination` · `trigger_or_target_gate`.

### The correction: OMNI already owns the right primitive, and has not applied it to itself

This needs **no new architecture**. It is `AB-12` — *the governance plane is self-hosted on the same primitives it governs* — applied reflexively. OMNI already separates **principal** (locus of authority and liability) from **actor** (who performs the act). Governance authority uses that same grammar:

| Object | What it is | Where it lives | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seat** | a named durable authority role with explicit **rights** and explicit **prohibitions** | **architecture** (binding) | changes only by architecture amendment |
| **Holder binding** | which principal occupies which seat, with effective dates | **a register** (mutable operational record) | changes by ordinary record edit — **not** an architecture change |
| **Grant** | a scoped, expiring delegation from a holder to an actor | runtime record | ordinary |

**The test this must pass:** *adding nine engineers, or three departments, or a compliance function, must be a register edit and must not touch a single architecture document.* Neither prior proposal passed it. This one does.

### Build-plane seat catalog

| Seat | May | May **never** |
|---|---|---|
| `cross_cutting_architecture_acceptance` | accept or reject cross-cutting architecture; record explicit override rationale | override a domain owner's truth or contract; accept a change it authored |
| `domain_owner_approval` | approve changes to owned domain truth, lifecycle, contract, commit authority; **block promotion into owned truth** | bind another domain; accept cross-cutting architecture alone |
| `architecture_steward` | maintain coherence; prepare cross-cutting decisions; run impact and correspondence analysis | override domain ownership; accept its own proposals |
| `independent_review` | produce **required** review evidence; force a **recorded disposition** | veto autonomously; commit; hold domain or repository authority |
| `proposal_authoring` | research, propose, author, test — **humans and agents alike** | accept, approve, or commit anything |
| `integration` | land an **already-accepted** coherent change set | resolve substantive disagreement by merging; decide content |
| `repository_administration` | apply hosted settings; issue the administration receipt (`C-07`) | decide architecture content |

**Occupancy rules — this is what makes it scale:**
- a seat may have **many** holders; a holder may hold **many** seats;
- **separation of duties:** the same actor may not hold `proposal_authoring` and `cross_cutting_architecture_acceptance` **for the same change**; `integration` may never be co-exercised with acceptance on the same change set;
- a seat with **zero** holders causes every gate depending on it to **fail closed** — never to silently default upward;
- **agent grants, for THIS transaction only:** proposal authoring, research, verification and testing. No agent holds cross-cutting acceptance, domain-owner approval or independent repository authority here. **This is an arc restriction, NOT a general OMNI rule** — the durable law is *"an agent never self-authorizes,"* and which action classes an agent may perform is a question of **grant and action class**, resolved at `§G1-AUTH`. *(R1 stated "agents may hold `proposal_authoring` only" as universal; **withdrawn at R2** — it contradicts the estate, which already contemplates agents performing bounded operational actions, conformance work and ministerial execution under delegated capability with point-of-consequence reauthorization.)*

**Disagreement rule.** A `domain_owner_approval` objection **blocks** promotion of a change into that owner's truth or contract. An `independent_review` objection **requires recorded disposition** but is **not** an autonomous veto. `integration` never resolves substantive disagreement by merging.

### Holder register — a fact, not a law

**Today:** one principal (the operator) holds `cross_cutting_architecture_acceptance`, `architecture_steward`, every `domain_owner_approval` seat, and `repository_administration`. The current external review instance holds `independent_review`. Repository-native and external agents hold `proposal_authoring`. **`integration` is VACANT** — which is exactly why no shared control-plane surface may land today, and is consistent with `PRESPINE-PHASEA-INTEGRATOR` being vacant.

**That concentration is a staffing fact with a recorded date. It is not the architecture, and the architecture must never assert it.**

**Naming a person is prohibited in an authority position; it is not prohibited in lineage.** *"The independent reviewer proposed X and it was accepted"* is provenance and should be preserved — that is how the estate records why a decision exists. *"Knox holds review authority"* is a **seat binding** and belongs in the register, never in an architecture document. The execution plan is compliant on this test: its four remaining named mentions are all lineage ("Knox's correction accepted", "Knox's split accepted"), and **the G0 authority table contains seats only.**

### Two populations, one grammar

| Plane | Population | Seat catalog |
|---|---|---|
| **Build plane** (meta — governs OMNI itself) | OMNI builders, reviewers, integrators, administrators **and their agents**; later: named departments and functions | the table above |
| **Use plane** (runtime — governed *by* OMNI) | patients, representatives and families, clinicians, staff, operators, federation members, external principals **and their agents** | domain-owned; **not** enumerated here |

**One authority grammar, two seat catalogs.** The populations are different and must never be conflated — but if the build plane invents a *second* authority mechanism, the two will diverge and OMNI will have to reconcile them later. That is the shape of the failure this arc exists to stop.

### Is an agent an actor, the same as a human?

The operator has asked this before and recorded that the answer kept flipping. **The estate already answers it, and PRE-0 independently confirms it.** `C4.4 §R` repeats the same clause across `R.1`–`R.17`:

> "**Agent-Runtime.** A runtime actor **may** be instantiated to *drive* a … mission — it **proposes + routes**; it never self-authorizes admission of high-consequence …; commit stays gated."

The settled answer, in one line each:

- **Agent = actor. Always.** So is a human, a device, a service. Acting is not authority.
- **Agent ≠ principal. Ever.** Liability does not attach to software.
- **An agent may hold a scoped, expiring delegated grant** — so it *is* a delegate. The blind design's "agents are instruments, never delegates" is **too absolute and is rejected**; OMNI already models delegated capability.
- **Every consequential agent act resolves to a non-null accountable principal**, a scoped capability, an authority basis, and a commit ceiling.
- **Symmetric across both planes.** A build-side agent authoring an amendment is an actor under a delegated grant with no acceptance authority — the *same grammar* as a care-side agent proposing a clinical action. This symmetry is the reason there is one grammar and not two.

**Consequence for this arc:** the repository-native agent that authored this ledger holds `proposal_authoring` and nothing else. It cannot accept G0. Neither can the review instance.
