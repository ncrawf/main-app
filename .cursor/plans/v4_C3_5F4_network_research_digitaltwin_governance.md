## Strategic reframe carried into this batch (Nick 2026-06-14 — sharpens F3's FS10)
F3 left an open thesis question ("operating-interface vs governed substrate"). Nick's device clarification resolves the *shape* of the answer and F4 adopts it as a **design invariant**:
- **OMNI owns the LINK + TELEMETRY + RECORD + the COMMAND/AUTHORITY LOOP — not the surface, and not the physical actuation.** OMNI binds to a vent / art-line / monitor / video / in-room button (via the manufacturer's API/MCP), ingests and owns its data, owns the *record* of every signal, and holds the *authority gate over which commands are permitted*.
- **Command authority is a per-device, per-context BOUNDARY:** `read-only` (e.g. an ICU vent that only streams in) · `bounded-control` (allowed in some instances, gated) · `prohibited` (most actuation, for now). OMNI does **not** learn/own the physics of the output (respiratory mechanics, CO₂→rate titration); the **device / robot / embedded operator governs actuation**. OMNI governs the *loop, the data, the authority, and the proof*.
- **Therefore the FS10 answer becomes:** *OMNI is a governed execution **substrate** that **owns the data+command loop wherever it links a signal source** — and earns surface/loop ownership by owning that loop, never by owning the actuation or cloning the UI.* (Full reframe in §FS-STRAT.)
