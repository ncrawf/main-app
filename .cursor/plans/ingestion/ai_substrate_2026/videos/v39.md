<Got it — this one is not central AI-agent architecture, but it is useful as a trust-boundary analogy.

The OMNI-relevant takeaway is:

A secure rail does not eliminate trust. It relocates trust.

For OMNI, that means API, MCP, A2A, VPN-like tunnel, partner integration, model service, data proxy, or agent runtime cannot be treated as “safe” just because the transport is encrypted or standardized. The real question is always:

Who can see what, who can act, who logs it, who can be compelled, who can misuse it, and who is responsible?

Useful concepts to preserve from this:

Header vs payload distinction
Payload may be protected, but metadata/routing/context can still leak. OMNI version: even if PHI content is protected, identity, intent, endpoint, operator, timing, and action lineage can still reveal sensitive context.
VPN = trust transfer
A VPN hides traffic from one party but concentrates trust in another. OMNI version: using an MCP server, vendor AI model, integration broker, or partner rail may reduce one risk while creating another.
Tor vs VPN = topology matters
Centralized trust is simpler/faster; distributed trust is more private/resilient but slower/complex. OMNI version: some workflows need fast centralized internal context; some high-risk cross-operator/federated workflows need stronger compartmentalization.
Use-case-specific routing
There is no universal “best.” Depends on threat model. OMNI version: context injection, retrieval, model choice, tool access, and external data routing need policy-based selection, not vibes.
Federation implication
This reinforces the prior point: Federation is not “integration wiring.” Federation is the trust/provenance/custody/visibility/authority layer that tells OMNI what a rail is allowed to mean.

So I’d tag this as:

Priority: medium-low
Where it lands: Federation, Boundary Policy, Agentic Consent, Context Router, Security/Runtime Governance
