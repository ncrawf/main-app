My updated view of OMNI
OMNI is no longer best described as a “healthcare platform” or even a “care operating system” in the simple SaaS sense.
The richer version is:
OMNI is a governed care/business execution substrate that coordinates sensing, reasoning, action, evidence, commerce, communication, and human authority across fragmented healthcare operators — with AI routed through domain-owned truth, source custody, policy, runtime controls, and audit.
That sounds dense, but it matters.
Because after the corpus, the center of gravity shifted away from “what features does OMNI have?” and toward:
What kind of operating system is required for AI to safely act inside healthcare and business workflows?
That is the real thesis.
Not intake.
 Not RAG.
 Not messaging.
 Not provider copilot.
 Not AI agents.
 Not dashboards.
 Not “medical super-app.”
Those are surfaces or capabilities.
The actual OMNI thesis is that healthcare needs a coordination substrate where:
source events become candidates
candidates are resolved through policy/context
domains commit truth
humans retain appropriate authority
actions produce evidence
evidence updates context
context improves future decisions
all of this remains federated, permissioned, observable, auditable, and economically sustainable
That thesis survived the 107-source pass.
Actually, it got stronger.
But the acceptable implementation bar got much higher.

The big shift from v2/v3 to v4
The older OMNI architecture was already strong on domain truth.
It had the right instincts:
D3 Scheduling is not D5 Service Occurrence.
D5 actualized work is not D7 documentation.
D6 money/entitlement is not clinical truth.
D7 artifact is not Observation.
Observation is not Clinical Memory.
Clinical Memory is not raw patient-source intake.
Messaging is not the decision substrate.
AI proposes; domains/humans commit.
Federation matters.
Candidate is not commit.
Projection is not authority.
That remains excellent.
The corpus did not invalidate that. It validated it.
But v2/v3 were weaker on several newer dimensions:
How agents actually get built and controlled.
How runtime/model/inference decisions become product decisions.
How connectors/MCP/tooling become attack surface and operational substrate.
How healthcare AI fails through burden, timing, thresholds, alert fatigue, and wrong metrics.
How evidence, multimodal artifacts, telemetry, video, transcript, OCR, and source lineage need ingestion discipline.
How security must become a continuous Build OS lane, not a later audit.
How architecture memory must compile into executable agent workflows.
How autonomous action requires consequence modeling, simulation, uncertainty, and recovery.
How cost/latency/quality lanes are part of the architecture, not deployment trivia.
So v4 should not replace v2/v3.
v4 should preserve the structural skeleton and add the missing operating layers.
A good way to say it:
v2/v3 defined the domain physics. v4 must define the execution physics.

4. Opus should identify v4 “thesis-stable” doctrine
Some things should become stronger in v4, not rewritten.
Domain-owned truth is still the backbone

Every serious source pointed away from one giant AI blob and toward modular, governed, tool-using systems.
That means the old OMNI insistence on domain ownership is not stale. It is ahead of the curve.
The strongest v4 doctrine should say:
AI may retrieve, reason, classify, draft, compare, simulate, and recommend. It does not commit domain truth. Every committed fact has one owning domain, one authority path, one audit trail, and one lifecycle.
That is a v4 anchor.
CNS is still valid
