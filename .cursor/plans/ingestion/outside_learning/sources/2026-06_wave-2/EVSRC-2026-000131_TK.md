Keeper doctrine

OMNI should learn from workflow traces, but every learning loop must define its environment, state, actions, feedback, reward, exploration policy, and promotion gate.

Second:

A static policy maps state to action. A learning agent updates its policy from feedback. OMNI should be a governed learning system, not merely static workflows.

Third:

Exploration is necessary for learning but dangerous in care/business contexts. High-risk exploration must move to sandbox, shadow mode, offline evals, or human-reviewed drafts.

Fourth:

Some constraints are not rewards. Clinical safety, consent, PHI, identity confidence, RBAC, and domain authority are blockers.

Fifth:

Model-based learning is preferred where authority, explainability, safety, and workflow physics matter. Model-free outcome learning is safer in constrained, measurable, low-risk domains.

Sixth:

Historical traces can teach better policy without being imitated blindly. Behavior-policy provenance matters.

Seventh:

Unknown states should usually reduce autonomy, not trigger random action.

Eighth:

OMNI operates under partial observability. Hidden state must be inferred carefully and kept separate from committed truth.

Ninth:

Reward must be long-horizon and multi-dimensional: safety, trust, revenue, speed, quality, compliance, provider confidence, patient clarity, and operator leverage.

Tenth:

Learning loops need observability, attribution, evals, and proof before promotion.

What not to import blindly

Do not turn OMNI into generic reinforcement learning.

Do not let expected utility override domain/human authority.

Do not randomly explore patient-facing, clinical, PHI, consent, pricing, or provider-authority workflows.

Do not treat Q-values or action-value estimates as permission.

Do not optimize on one scalar reward without preserving value dimensions.

Do not imitate human behavior without quality/outcome/authority labels.

Do not let unknown states crash or choose random actions.

Do not assume fully observed state; OMNI is mostly partially observed.

Do not use model-free learning for high-risk clinical/business seams without a domain/world model.

Do not confuse “agent” in RL lecture with OMNI’s production agent/CNS architecture without translating carefully.

Do-not-miss lesson

This source gives OMNI the cleanest basic feedback-loop law:

A learning system needs a defined environment, explicit actions, observable feedback, reward signals, and policy updates. In OMNI, that loop must be constrained by safety, authority, consent, evidence, evals, and long-horizon trust.

Shortest OMNI version:

Rollouts are traces. Feedback updates policy only through gates. Explore where safe, never randomly in high-risk care. Build world models for consequential workflows. Use outcome learning for lower-risk patterns. Treat rewards as governed design, not magic math. Domains and humans still commit truth.
