Keeper doctrine

Responsible AI is not ethics wallpaper. It is rigor infrastructure for AI research, product, evals, and deployment.

Second:

Rigor has at least six facets: epistemic, normative, conceptual, methodological, reporting, and interpretative. Method alone is not enough.

Third:

A technically functioning model can still be built on an invalid task, bad assumption, pseudoscience, or unsupported construct.

Fourth:

Constructs must be defined before they are measured, automated, optimized, or claimed.

Fifth:

Aggregated metrics hide tail failures. OMNI must disaggregate by role, risk, context, surface, population, and workflow class.

Sixth:

Claims must stay inside the evidence. Do not turn narrow benchmark/pilot results into broad capability or trust claims.

Seventh:

Transparency matters because it supports human agency, not because transparency itself is the goal.

Eighth:

Human agency requires mechanisms: choice, control, consent, correction, remediation, disclosure, overreliance prevention, and human fallback.

Ninth:

AI personas can support brainstorming but cannot replace actual stakeholder participation or user research.

Tenth:

Anthropomorphic AI is a risk surface, especially in healthcare, mental health, children, older adults, body image, coaching, and vulnerable contexts.

Eleventh:

Patient-facing AI should be humane without pretending to be human.

Twelfth:

Responsible AI must apply to commerce/growth AI as much as clinical/support AI.

What not to import blindly

Do not reduce responsible AI to ethics language.

Do not treat “we ran evals” as proof of rigor if constructs/claims are muddy.

Do not use AI personas as a replacement for real users, patients, providers, staff, or operators.

Do not claim “trust,” “agency,” “safety,” “behavior change,” or “clinical quality” without defining and measuring them.

Do not rely on aggregate metrics for safety.

Do not make broad claims from narrow benchmarks.

Do not use “hallucination” or other AI terms without clarifying meaning.

Do not make AI companions too human-like in care contexts.
