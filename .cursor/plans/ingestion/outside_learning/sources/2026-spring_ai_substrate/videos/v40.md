## 2. ChatGPT distillation

<Yes — this one is highly relevant. Higher priority than the VPN one.

The core OMNI takeaway:

Human involvement is not a yes/no safety feature. It is a runtime authority mode.

This transcript gives us a clean vocabulary OMNI needs:

HITL — human-in-the-loop:
AI stops and waits for approval before proceeding.

HOTL — human-on-the-loop:
AI acts, but a human monitors and can override/kill-switch.

HOOTL — human-out-of-the-loop:
AI acts autonomously without live human intervention.

The important distinction is that humans can enter the system at three different layers:

Training time — humans label or correct data.
Tuning time — humans express preferences/judgment, like RLHF.
Inference/runtime — humans approve, monitor, override, or escalate live actions.

For OMNI, this means we should not simply say “AI requires provider review” or “AI can automate this.” We need an explicit AI authority envelope per workflow/action:

What can AI propose?
What can AI draft?
What can AI execute?
When must it stop?
When can it proceed but remain monitored?
When is full autonomy allowed?
What evidence allows a workflow to graduate from HITL → HOTL → HOOTL?

That last part is the 1BN-company piece. The goal is not to keep humans in every loop forever. The goal is to use humans strategically until the system earns trust through evals, traces, confidence thresholds, audit history, and low failure rates.

Where this lands in OMNI:

Runtime AI / CNS

action confidence thresholds
clinical-risk interrupt
escalation queues
approval gates
override / kill switch
“AI proposes, deterministic CNS validates, authorized human/domain commits”

Clinical / Medical

diagnosis, medication, contraindication, risk escalation stay HITL or tightly HOTL
low-risk summarization/drafting may be AI-assisted but not self-authoritative
clinical adoption remains human/domain-owned

Messaging / Intake

AI can classify, draft, extract, route, and suggest
sending, clinical advice, external commitments, refunds, scheduling commitments, or PHI-sensitive disclosures require action-specific authority envelopes

coding agents need the same model:
read-only/research actions can be HOOTL

schema/migration/auth/security changes require explicit approval gates

Governance / Learning

every human approval/rejection should become structured feedback, not just a one-off click
review outcomes feed evals, policy refinement, prompt/tool updates, and possibly future autonomy graduation
