>

Like — there's still nothing confirmed that all 500 of those people had that disease. But it was valuable info back to ME as clinician: hmm, 500 other docs ordered this too... okay, this seems like we're on the right track, we'll hedge it and actually treat — with Bactrim here! That's not a normal choice for profuse diarrhea out of the blue.
>
> What I'm wondering about is the mechanics of that interchange. It caused me to remark to `[PT]`: **"this is clinical medicine, there is no textbook answer here."** So imagine — sure, the provider may be right, wrong, hedging a bet, not hedging a bet, overruling an AI rec, acknowledging an AI rec, but having to pivot based on real-world resources or events, or insurance didn't cover it, having to quarterback between patient and pharmacy/pharmacist resources, all in real time. **AI needs to stay abreast of all that**, account for all that complexity, account for an unknown diagnosis with multiple potential paths forward — e.g. we do a stool study and wait, or we treat now and plan to get a stool study if able, or we treat empirically, or we treat something and treat with something targeted.
>
> A prescription for Bactrim can be: a broad guess, a bad guess, a good guess, an empiric guess, a near-certain targeted guess. It can be **all** of those, and still be number 2 below the quinolone guesses. It can be all those, and be down one path for potential care while sitting elsewhere on a different tree.
>
> Other thoughts: the real-time nature of all this. In 2030 the exchange may be verbal, sure, but AI needs to **monitor the conversation almost in real time**, and feed the provider like a feed — like an earpiece for a news anchor — into their ear: "oh, we see that happening, let's keep you up to date here, let's anticipate, let's stay abreast." I don't know what the name is for that type of interaction, but it's similar to spoken — but like we said in earlier videos, we need to anticipate new info coming in, cutoffs, redirects, non-clinical / side conversations... in written, in image, in verbal back-and-forth, between **provider ↔ patient ↔ AI ↔ outside-influence.** Like, all 4 of those actors had input for **ONE SIMPLE PRESCRIPTION WRITE.** That is the physics of care. > You are noticing that we normally overload a single word—"decision"—with several independent dimensions.
