Testosterone (TRT)

Not automatically lifelong, but often becomes long-term.

When you give exogenous testosterone:

LH and FSH are suppressed.
Intratesticular testosterone falls.
Endogenous testosterone production decreases, sometimes to near zero.
Spermatogenesis decreases.

If you stop TRT:

Many men recover endogenous production over weeks to months.
Some take 6–12 months.
Older men or those with underlying primary hypogonadism may never fully recover to baseline.
Sometimes a PCT (hCG ± clomiphene/enclomiphene) is used to stimulate recovery, though evidence varies.

So patients should be counseled that while TRT is not irreversible, they should approach it as a potentially long-term therapy.

Enclomiphene

Different story.

Enclomiphene stimulates the hypothalamus/pituitary to increase LH and FSH, so the testes make more testosterone naturally.

Advantages:

Preserves fertility better than TRT.
Usually maintains testicular size.
Doesn't suppress the axis the way TRT does.

If you stop it:

Testosterone generally returns to whatever the patient's baseline was.
It doesn't permanently "fix" hypogonadism.
If the underlying problem persists, symptoms usually return.

So it's not lifelong, but if it works and the underlying issue remains, many patients continue taking it.

hCG

Also generally not lifelong by necessity.

hCG acts like LH.

Stimulates Leydig cells.
Maintains intratesticular testosterone.
Helps preserve fertility.

Uses:

Alone (less common)
With TRT for fertility preservation
To help recover the HPG axis after TRT

Stopping hCG simply removes that stimulation. Endogenous function returns to whatever the patient's baseline capability is.

In your patient's case

This is exactly why I probably wouldn't start TRT first based on these labs alone. His free and bioavailable testosterone are normal despite a borderline total testosterone driven by low SHBG.

The key point is that none of these therapies permanently cure low testosterone. They either replace testosterone (TRT) or stimulate endogenous production (enclomiphene/hCG). When they're discontinued, hormone levels generally drift back toward the patient's underlying baseline.

knox  = Think of serum testosterone as being in three buckets:

Strongly bound to SHBG — generally not readily available to tissues.
Loosely bound to albumin — can dissociate easily; considered bioavailable.
Free testosterone — unbound and immediately available.

Total testosterone = all three buckets combined.

The key correction is: low SHBG usually lowers total testosterone more than it lowers free testosterone. It does not typically cause low free testosterone by itself.

What happens when SHBG is low

There are fewer binding sites holding testosterone in circulation. The body reaches a new equilibrium:

Less testosterone is SHBG-bound.
Total measured testosterone falls.
A larger percentage of the remaining testosterone is free.
Absolute free testosterone may stay normal.

That is basically his pattern:

Total T: 253
