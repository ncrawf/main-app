One recommendation on the prescription workflow

I would avoid making the medication itself a standard Boulevard "service" because you're not selling an in-office procedure—you're evaluating a patient and, if appropriate, prescribing a medication that's dispensed by a pharmacy. Instead:

Bill the consultation in Boulevard (or waive it, as in this case).
Record the treatment plan in the chart.
Collect payment for the medication only after you decide to prescribe it (either as a Boulevard retail item/custom charge or through whatever payment workflow you adopt).
Send the prescription to the 503B/compounding pharmacy for direct shipment.

That keeps the clinical encounter, prescribing decision, and medication fulfillment as distinct steps, which is generally a cleaner operational model and easier to scale as you add TRT, GLP-1s, female HRT, ED therapy, and other medical programs.

Do not routinely waive the follow-up

A follow-up is where you:

Assess efficacy and adverse effects
Review labs
Adjust dose or treatment
Decide whether to continue therapy
Renew or change medication
Document ongoing medical decision-making

That is not free administrative work. It is actual physician or advanced-practice clinical care.

National telehealth competitors commonly recover their clinical costs through recurring fees rather than truly providing free follow-up. Ro currently charges $149 monthly for ongoing weight-management care, excluding medication; Mochi’s standard membership is $79 monthly; Hone’s premium hormone/longevity model is $155 monthly plus medication and includes physician consultations, testing, and periodic follow-up.
