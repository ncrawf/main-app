### §B.1 — Observed-actor privacy vs oversight: the surveillance-asymmetry adoption paradox (`DTP-02` + `DTP-11`)

**The finding (operator's own words, load-bearing — preserve, do not flatten):**
> *"it's SAFE for me to use my external chatgpt with screenshots and swear at it… and say whatever… I know I'm not being watched. In OMNI, the whole idea is… we're watching everything. We have to anticipate what that will mean for patients, for providers, for workers… OMNI must have a STRONG STRONG stance."*

**Why this is genuinely new (dedup verdict): PARTIALLY-COVERED substrate, GENUINELY-NEW framing + doctrine gap.**
What the estate already has (do NOT re-mint):
- Patient-side privacy: consent-does-not-aggregate (§7.5.4), per-operator `visibility_grant`, portable artifacts, `degraded_safety_state`.
- **Network Governance Plane** (§7.6): **aggregate-by-default**; identifiable access only via **named access purposes** (support / safety incident / compliance audit / break-glass / explicit grant / contractual oversight); minimum-necessary, logged, reviewable.
- **Anti-realself (§13.6):** rejects doctor-popularity/surveillance scoring.
- **Workforce-as-subject guard (§8):** staff context is governed operating context, **purpose/role-scoped, audited — not covert surveillance**.
- **Accountable context envelope (GRR §9a):** "minimum sufficient, not a surveillance blob."

**What is NOT in the estate (the novel gap):** the estate treats provider/worker observation as *a governance-and-ethics constraint to bound*. It has **never framed observation as an ADOPTION and BEHAVIORAL force** — i.e., that:
1. **The unwatched-ness of the external tool is a *feature* to the provider.** Terseness, profanity, raw thinking, half-formed hypotheses, "bonkers shit," even impaired/erratic states — the provider externalizes precisely *because* the generalist AI does not record, judge, or expose. This is not a bug in the workaround; it is its value proposition against OMNI.
2. **OMNI's core property (govern + record + prove everything) is therefore a structural driver back toward externalization** — a direct tension with the whole thesis. The better OMNI's lineage, the stronger the incentive to do the "raw" thinking somewhere unwatched and only bring the clean output in. **This re-creates the exact screenshot-loop OMNI exists to displace, from the inside.**
3. **There is no observed-actor (provider/worker) privacy doctrine** parallel to patient consent-specificity. The Network Governance Plane governs *institutional* access to worker context; it does not answer *"what of the provider's own reasoning/scratch space is private from the business owner / OMNI admin / CMO / discovery, and by what scope/purpose/duration/recipient?"*

**Candidate landing (propose-only — routes like a G4 finding, `GRD-043`):**
- **A candidate NEW cross-cutting doctrine: `observed_actor_privacy` (working handle).** Symmetric to patient consent-specificity but for *any watched actor* (provider, staff, operator, agent). Its core question: for each observation surface, **who is watched · by whom · for what named purpose · retained how long · usable for what** — and, crucially, a **protected reasoning/scratch space** class where the actor can think rawly *within* OMNI without it becoming performance-review or discovery fodder by default. This is the in-substrate answer to "swear at the AI safely." Composes over §A (permeability) + RBAC visibility scopes + the Network Governance Plane's named-purpose machinery + D7 retention — it is a **new doctrine axis, not a new domain** (per `GRD-035` no domain owns a cross-cutting concern).
- **The stance itself is a first-class thesis posture (`DTP-11`).** "OMNI must take a strong stance" is not decoration: an explicit, legible observed-actor-privacy stance is an **adoption + trust lever**, the counter-move to the externalization gravity. Candidate spine line (propose-only): *OMNI makes actors safe to be observed by making the observation bounded, purpose-named, and non-punitive by default — the way it makes patients safe to be recorded.* Ties to the bet-memo membrane law (open rails / closed authority) and the "honest gravity / continuity-without-captivity" frame.
- **Design consequence:** OMNI likely needs a **governed private reasoning surface** (a provider/worker sandbox that is inside the substrate — so it *can* be governed, evidence-linked, and safety-checked — but whose contents are **not** default-visible to operator admin / network governance / routine discovery, only under a named purpose + gate). This is the in-substrate replacement for "screenshot into ChatGPT and swear at it." Connects to Agent Runtime `agent_session` (a private principal workspace, §0.5 `resolution_participation_binding` `workspace_scope: private_principal_workspace`) and the provider-sidebar (Care §9a) — but with an explicit **privacy scope**, which those currently lack.

**Pressure/counter-pressure (for Knox):**
- *Counter:* clinical safety + medico-legal record may REQUIRE that some provider reasoning is captured (you can't have a "private" space that hides negligent care). **Resolution direction:** the scratch space is *governed* (safety-checkable, evidence-linked, replayable **under a named purpose/break-glass**) but **not casually surveilled** — the same "aggregate-by-default, identifiable-by-named-purpose" logic the Network Governance Plane already uses for patients, now applied to the provider's own reasoning. Privacy ≠ non-existence; it's purpose-bound access. This keeps it inside the estate's grain.
- *Counter:* is this just RBAC visibility scopes? **No** — RBAC governs who-can-see-what-data; this is a doctrine about **whether a watched actor has a protected class of self-directed reasoning at all**, which is a *policy stance* RBAC would then enforce. The stance is the novel part.

### §B.2 — Audit-as-competence-indictment + liability distribution (`DTP-01`)

**The finding (operator's words):**
> *"what if the audit later shows the provider didn't know what they were doing, was misled by the AI, and carried it out? Is that auditable? A legal issue — for whom? Lawyer: 'we looked at your queries.' CMO: 'do you not understand T therapy?'"*

**Why this is genuinely new (dedup verdict): PARTIALLY-NEW — the inverse of an existing mechanic.**
What the estate already has:
- **`ai_decision_log` (3 orthogonal axes: exposure/disposition × influence/use × later-eval)** — tracks AI→human influence (§0.5 ③ F3; regulation-forced: EU AI Act + TX TRAIGA).
- **`rubber_stamp_guard` / review-meaningfulness audit** (EVSRC-149, forensic Lane 5 T72 OPEN) — catches non-inspectable HITL.
- **Accountable context envelope** (GRR §9a) — version-at-time, minimum-sufficient.
- **C3.8 breaker-8 (OPEN):** BYOM liability split (provider / OMNI / federation) is an *acknowledged-open* decision.
- **Replayable proof** + REV-184 outcome-reads-original-context (outcome doesn't retroactively brand a reasonable decision negligent).

**What is NOT in the estate (the novel gap):** the estate tracks AI-influence to *protect* the decision (disclosure, override rationale, "don't let outcome rewrite reasoning"). It has **not** framed the **reverse**: OMNI's own lineage becomes **discoverable evidence that can indict the human** —
1. **Audit-as-competence-indictment.** The very completeness that makes OMNI trustworthy makes it a discovery goldmine: "we looked at your queries — you asked the AI a question that shows you didn't understand T therapy, it misled you, and you carried it out." The governed record can be turned *against* the provider (and against OMNI/federation) in court or CMO admin. The ungoverned ChatGPT has **no such record** — which is, again, why the workaround feels safe (this is the DIRECT bridge to §B.1: the two surfaces are one).
2. **Liability distribution is unspecified.** When AI-influence lineage exists and shows "provider was misled by AI and carried it out," *who bears it?* Provider (carried it out) / OMNI (the substrate surfaced the misleading candidate) / federation (deployed the model/policy) / the model vendor? C3.8 breaker-8 flags the split as open; this specimen makes it **concrete and care-facing**, not just enterprise-BYOM.
3. **The record's dual nature.** The same envelope is *shield* (proves the provider acted reasonably on what was known) AND *sword* (proves incompetence/misleading-and-carried-out). OMNI must be explicit about **which is default and under what access purpose the sword can be drawn** — which routes straight back to §B.1's observed-actor-privacy stance (who may run the competence-indicting query, for what named purpose).

**Candidate landing (propose-only):**
- **Sharpen `ai_decision_log` to carry a competence/comprehension dimension explicitly** — not just "was the AI influential?" but "does the *disposition record* expose the human's comprehension state?" and gate who can read that dimension for what purpose (ties to §B.1). Candidate semantic (name TBD at C5): the influence-lineage log has a **discovery-exposure class** per field.
- **A liability-attribution frame at the commit gate** — when an `authorized_action` was AI-influenced, the resolution record should carry an explicit **accountability-attribution** (who committed, on what evidence, with what AI-influence, under what authority) so the split is *decidable from the record* rather than argued after the fact. Consumes REV-184 (frozen context, outcome-reads-original-context) + accountable-context-envelope + the C3.8 tenant/BYOM liability posture. **This is a candidate C3.8-breaker-8 follow-on**, made care-concrete.
- **The stance (ties §B.1 + §DTP-11):** OMNI's audit is **non-punitive by default and purpose-bound to be drawn** — the same governance that protects patients from casual PHI inspection protects providers from casual competence-surveillance, while preserving break-glass for genuine safety/legal need. Without this stance, providers rationally flee to the unwatched tool.

**Pressure (for Knox):** this cannot become "OMNI hides provider error." It's the opposite grain: the record exists and is complete (that's the value), but *access to the competence-indicting reading is purpose-bound and logged*, and the record's default reading is the **shield** (what-was-known-then), not the **sword**. This is exactly the estate's existing patient-side logic (aggregate-by-default, identifiable-by-named-purpose) generalized to the observed provider — which is why §B.1 and §B.2 are one doctrine, not two.
