10. Strongest final self-attack
The final laws may still be too abstract

F1, F2, F4, and F6 are predominantly negative constraints:

do not infer financing from care;
do not equate distinct positions;
do not rewrite historical incidence;
do not pretend a financial reversal undoes care.

Those can reject bad architectures without identifying a positive architectural boundary. They may be constitutional review questions rather than “physics” capable of supporting ownership reconciliation.

F3 is close to a resource-conservation statement. If it belongs to general resource physics, it adds no financing-specific layer.

F5 is a projection rule.

F7 is a normative traceability choice.

On this attack, almost nothing remains that requires an autonomous care-financing construct.

The final model may remain over-unified

F2 places willingness, entitlement, commitment, obligation, reservation, transfer, and settlement in one sentence. That may create a false family resemblance.

The only thing they may share is that someone can use them while reasoning about care. That is not enough to justify:

one lifecycle;
one common record;
one service;
one authority;
or one interface.

Even “historical incidence” may differ too much among money, labor, capacity, debt, and pool depletion to justify common ownership.

The final model may merely rename familiar machinery

Neutral terms do not guarantee independence from incumbent assumptions:

“commitment” can become authorization under a new name;
“recognized responsibility” can become adjudication;
“source composition” can become coordination among payers;
“historical incidence” can become payment history;
“care-impact assertion” can become a renamed financial clearance.

If those terms are implemented around one case, one amount, one adjudicator, and one eventual responsible party, the analysis will have reproduced familiar machinery while claiming abstraction.

The final model may be architecturally unnecessary

The inherited substrate may already be sufficient:

care authorities own care assertions;
rights authorities own entitlements;
obligors and adjudicators own obligations;
resource controllers own capacity facts;
commerce authorities own transfers and settlement;
all preserve attribution, time, uncertainty, and correction history.

A care workflow can consume explicitly non-authoritative projections from those sources. No financing-specific authoritative layer may be required.

Invalidation condition

The conclusion of minimal shared physics is invalidated if either of the following is established:

Too little commonality: every surviving law either dissolves completely into general authority, resource, obligation, commerce, temporal, and care-continuity laws or remains only a local projection, with no recurring authoritative cross-regime behavior.
More commonality than found: materially different regimes repeatedly require the same authoritative creation, correction, composition, incidence, and closure lifecycle, and representing that lifecycle once demonstrably preserves semantics better than source-native alternatives without creating a coordinator or god-object.

The first result would eliminate care-financing physics as a distinct architectural concern. The second would show that this Phase-B conclusion remained too minimal.
