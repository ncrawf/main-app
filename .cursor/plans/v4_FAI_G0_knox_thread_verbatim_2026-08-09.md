A-Q18 — payer-mandated pharmacy is a funding condition

admissibility/capability | availability | preference | policy precedence | funding_condition | execution rail | objective | incentive lineage | authorized selector.

The three-law firewall becomes implementable
The most important Gate-2 product mechanism, in my view, is a two-stage decision boundary:

Stage 1 — clinical meaning. Determine clinical indication, acceptability, equivalence, contraindication, recommendation, and professional options under the appropriate clinical authority. Interested-principal economics must not alter this stage.

Stage 2 — feasibility composition. Overlay patient affordability/preferences, coverage, payer funding conditions, logistics, availability, lawful substitutions, timing, credit, and other resource/access facts. This stage may change what is feasible or what the patient accepts, but any resulting plan change must retain a separately attributed resource/access-driven rationale rather than rewriting Stage 1.

That simultaneously enforces all three inherited laws: payment state does not author clinical state; interested-party economics cannot masquerade as clinical merit; patient economic reality remains visible and may legitimately constrain feasibility.

The audit mechanism should test read sets, not merely final prose: mutate operator margin, lender origination revenue, manufacturer economics, or payer cost while holding clinical evidence fixed. The clinically ranked option set must remain invariant. Patient affordability or a genuine funding condition may change the feasible/accepted set, but the trace must show that as a different reason class.

That is much stronger than “tell the model not to be biased.”
