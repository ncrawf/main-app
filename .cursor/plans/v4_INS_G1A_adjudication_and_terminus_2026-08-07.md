**The split that matters most.** F1, F2, F3, F4, F6 are presented as **structural**; **F5 and F7 are normative commitments requiring explicit architectural acceptance rather than being disguised as unavoidable physics.** That separation is exactly what the protocol's normativity test was built to force, and it worked.

### §2.1 — The constitutional statement was attacked and split

Our Gate-0 formulation was too broad. The independent agent split it into two different laws:

1. **Historical integrity — INHERITED.** A later financing or resource assertion cannot retroactively replace an earlier care-side assertion, recommendation, consent state or rationale. A legitimate later change is a **new attributed statement.**
2. **Material care-impact reliance — CANDIDATE.** When a care decision materially relies on a financing or resource assertion, the source, authority, scope, relevant times, uncertainty and material effect must remain **reconstructable separately from the care-side decision**, subject to visibility rights.

**Why this is more exact than what we had.** It permits a patient or clinician to *legitimately revise a decision* after learning financial reality, while prohibiting silent historical rewriting and false attribution. Our version ("financing must not silently rewrite…") conflated the inherited law with the candidate one and could have been read as forbidding legitimate revision.
