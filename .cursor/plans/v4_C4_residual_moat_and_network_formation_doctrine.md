## §6 — Firewall = license; neutrality = constitutional participation condition (NOT the moat); the healthcare asymmetry; self-binding on OMNI (FSG-03 + the neutrality rework)

- **The firewall (clinical/commercial separation, no self-preference smuggling) is OMNI's *license to form the network*** — table stakes to be allowed to sit between counterparties. Copyable; not a moat.
- **Neutrality is NOT the moat, and NOT mere acquisition scaffolding.** (Gemini was right that markets tolerate self-preference when utility is high — AWS/Retail, Epic Care Everywhere, Apple; nobody adopts neutrality for virtue. But Gemini's "neutrality is only an acquisition tactic" is wrong.) Neutrality is the **constitutional participation condition** that does three jobs: (i) *participation enabler* — competing/independent parties join because they need not surrender authority or alpha; (ii) *governance constitution* — it constrains how routing, ranking, learning, economics, and proof behave *after* the network forms; (iii) *legitimacy mechanism* — it reduces the risk that density turns OMNI into the extractive aggregator it exists to counter.

**The healthcare pressure asymmetry (LEGAL/ECONOMIC HYPOTHESIS — counsel validation required; Knox #5 de-overclaim):** in AWS/Epic/Apple the party eating the conflict-cost is a rival *seller*. In medicine, conflicted routing sits inside **stronger and more varied legal, professional, fiduciary, safety, and reputational pressures** than ordinary retail — so *auditable* neutrality may be strategically valuable in particular rails and arrangements. **This does NOT create a universal legal mandate for neutral healthcare networks.** The relevant statutes have distinct, narrower scopes: the federal **Anti-Kickback Statute** concerns knowing/willful remuneration to induce or reward *federal-healthcare-program* referrals/business (with safe harbors); **Stark** generally concerns physician referrals for designated health services where a financial relationship exists (with exceptions); **corporate-practice-of-medicine** rules are *state-specific* and typically target control of professional judgment/ownership (e.g. California guidance is one state's, not a generic platform-neutrality law); **information-blocking** rules govern practices interfering with EHI access/exchange/use by defined actors — **none of these establishes general platform or network neutrality.** This matters acutely because the initial wedge is substantially **cash-pay**, where federal-program referral law may not apply the same way. **Applicability must be resolved per jurisdiction · service · payment regime · ownership structure · referral relationship · participant role.** Neutrality may be a commercial + governance advantage; **do NOT call it a "liability shield" or a legally durable moat before arrangement-specific counsel review.** (Home: the regulatory-operating-physics watch item; legal-as-dynamic-authority-layer.)

**Self-binding (the honest half):** OMNI runs commerce (D6) and holds AI-supply relationships, so **OMNI faces the same self-preference temptation as it scales.** Neutrality is therefore *also a leash on OMNI*, not only a weapon against incumbents. Write it as both or it is propaganda.

- **The moat emerges only when the firewall + neutrality become externally legible · contractually relied upon · independently auditable · economically durable · proven across many counterparties.** → **Earned neutral acceptance is the *network-layer moat hypothesis*** — and even then the *defensible layer* is the accumulated operated system around acceptance (§7), not the acceptance grammar itself (which may become an open standard, a participation threshold, a regulatory expectation, or shared infrastructure).
- **Falsifier:** stays a hypothesis until counterparties **behave differently because they rely on it.** Acceptance without a loved product is standards bureaucracy.

**Define the moved-toward asset precisely — trusted-execution liquidity is MULTIDIMENSIONAL, never a scalar** (it must not become a single network score through the back door — that is exactly the §8 non-commensurability rule): qualified counterparties available by capability + jurisdiction · probability + latency of authorized acceptance · execution success rate · exception + remedy performance · current adapter/rail coverage · capacity + service-level fit · contractual reliability · ability to multi-home · transparent participation + exit.

In healthcare, coercive captivity is not even a viable moat — it is a liability (regulatory, reputational, patient-harm). This also answers "the product must commoditize" (§9): the product defends on care outcomes + labor economics regardless of the network. It moves when OMNI creates **measurable counterparty value or removes a real constraint.** Proof is a **layered stack, not a magic token:** *technical* (what occurred, who signed, which version, what custody was accepted) → *contractual* (what obligations/liabilities that proof activates) → *economic* (payment timing, reimbursement, fees, guarantees) → *clinical/legal* (what a party may safely/lawfully rely upon). **Cryptography establishes identity/integrity/sequence/non-repudiation; it does NOT indemnify — indemnity is contracts, warranties, insurance, reserves.**

**The N=1 cold-start is NOT solved — it is a BRANCHING HYPOTHESIS (Knox #8; §0.5-C; the most important open business/architecture fork).** At density, volume-pull works (OMNI brings qualified demand the counterparty wants). At N=1 an independent operator has no volume to force a large counterparty, and the tempting answer — "OMNI relieves a constraint on the counterparty's existing book" — hides a fork:
> *If the benefit applies **only to OMNI-originated transactions**, the economics scale with OMNI volume and the cold-start problem is unsolved. If it improves the counterparty's **broader existing book**, OMNI has quietly expanded into a **counterparty-side operations product** (pharmacy workflow / conformance / audit tooling) — possibly strategically correct, but a far larger product + authority decision than a moat sentence may assume.*

**At N=1, participation must be bootstrapped through one or more of FOUR mechanisms (branching, not a solved lever):**
1. **operator-funded value** on OMNI-originated transactions (economics scale with OMNI volume — the honest cold-start cost);
2. **anchor-counterparty co-development or contractual commitment** (a design partner seeds acceptance);
3. **a broader counterparty-side operations product** that improves the counterparty's existing book (explicitly a NEW product/authority surface — decide it on purpose, don't smuggle it in);
4. **a native or mandated rail** that lets OMNI *bridge* without requiring the counterparty to change its workflow.

> **Live-state honesty (`v4_C4_6_...` pressure test — C4.6 G2 frozen):** external counterparty acceptance is **unproven**, external interoperability ~**5% built**, market mechanics largely **unresolved**, the pharmacy network mostly **design, not production**. ## §14 — The OPEN captivity / gravity tension (expanded taxonomy + operational portability tests; the honest proof this is not settled — open-review row)

The bet memo §6 condemns gravity-from-hostage-data, yet a naïve $10B moat could bank on *compounding proprietary relational history.* The §7 clearinghouse framing is the *proposed* resolution (liquidity, not captivity) — but it is **not yet closed**, and an adjacent mechanism lives in the same question: *governed longitudinal coherence as the fuel that makes replaceable models perform irreplaceably* (the AI-collapsed institution) — legitimate gravity or captivity trap?

**The single unresolved question (open-review row `D0-REV-010`, general namespace — deliberately NOT a new `D0STRAT` namespace):**
> *When does governed longitudinal continuity create **legitimate, portable, permissioned gravity** — and when does accumulated relational history become **captivity or extraction**?*

**Closure requires explicitly separating the asset classes:**
1. **patient-portable** — facts, artifacts, consent, provenance, commitment history;
2. **operator-private** — relationship history + local alpha;
3. **counterparty-native** — the counterparty's own execution truth;
4. **OMNI-generalizable** — conformance patterns, failure knowledge, evals, remedy competence;
5. **permissioned network** — acceptance / proof / participation evidence;
6. **OMNI first-party** — product know-how;
7. **PROHIBITED** — alpha-laundering · non-portable hostage history · extraction · records defensible only because leaving destroys continuity.

**And it must pass operational tests (the clearinghouse promise made checkable):**
- Can a **patient** leave without losing clinically necessary continuity?
- Can an **operator** leave without OMNI transferring its private playbook to competitors?
- Can **counterparties** retain their native execution truth?
- Which proof is **exportable**? Which learned capability legitimately belongs to **OMNI**?
- Which network effects survive **full lawful portability** (multi-homing, fair access, non-discrimination)?
- Is the remaining defensibility **performance + trust + live liquidity** — or merely **reconstruction cost** (= captivity)?

**Captivity can ALSO arise WITHOUT withholding data — through network-operator RULEMAKING + market power (Knox #11; this is where neutrality-as-self-binding, §6, becomes real or fails):** exclusive participation · discriminatory routing · take-rate escalation · unilateral rule changes · opaque rankings · arbitrary suspension/expulsion · non-portable credentials · dependency on proprietary identity/proof services · no appeal · inability to multi-home. **Operational tests for rule / market-power captivity:**
- Who sets participation + conformance rules? Who may change fees?
- Can OMNI privilege its own products or partners? What prevents OMNI's first-party products from receiving privileged network treatment?
- What notice + appeal exist for rule changes or expulsion?
- Can participants **multi-home without retaliation**? Are similarly-situated counterparties treated consistently?
- Can an **independent body audit** OMNI's routing + self-preference?
- What governance rights do **patients, operators, providers, and counterparties** hold?

**The network operator's rulemaking power may become more consequential than its data custody.** This is the concrete content of §6's "self-binding": the constitution must bind OMNI's *rules and market power*, not merely its data handling — otherwise a data-portable network can still become captive at the level of participation terms.

Standard: **exit without destruction; stay because the system is better.** Losing OMNI-operated *network services* on exit is legitimate; withholding a participant's *own* truth/rights/authority/continuity to punish departure is not. Until this closes, this frame **cannot** call itself the settled OMNI moat.
