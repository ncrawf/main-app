## What OMNI is

OMNI is a **governed contextual care substrate**.

It maintains longitudinal coherence — through identity, consent, authority, evidence, observation, assertion, policy, coordination, and execution primitives — across a designed family of care-network topologies that expands by explicit decision, not by drift.

The substrate is **future-permissive**. The product is **brutally narrow and concrete**.

**What OMNI replaces (product scope — Lens A):** in one governed substrate, what a clinic/network today stitches from a dozen siloed SaaS products — **Hims/Ro** (telehealth+Rx) · **Hims-Labs/Quest** (labs) · **Epic/Cerner** (EMR) · **Shopify** (commerce) · **WordPress** (site/content) · **Mindbody** (scheduling/memberships) · **RingCentral** (comms) · **ActiveCampaign** (marketing) · **Oracle/Workday/QuickBooks** (back-office) · **Stripe** (payments) · and the **ChatGPT-screenshot-then-confirm** workaround it displaces. The win isn't feature-parity; it's running them all on one substrate sharing identity/consent/authority/evidence.

**How OMNI behaves (Lens B — borrowed concepts):** **Amazon** (ordered things→fulfillment→exceptions = Act loop) · **Tesla** (signals→state→safety-monitor→feedback = Sense loop) · **NASA/Houston** (telemetry-vs-command, authority gates, go/no-go, audit) · **Airport/ATC** (scheduling/routing/queues/capacity = Planning) · **Airplane-as-object** (redundancy, checklists, fail-safe, black-box) · **Stripe** (idempotency, immutable audit, payment-state separation) · **FHIR/HL7** (structured ingestion) · **Anthropic** (the build-OS engineering discipline — doctrine-first, guardrails, decision records, review gates) · **Apple HealthKit** (contributions-to-a-record review queue) · **Salesforce/Zendesk** (one-interaction→many-intents) · **Mirth/Rhapsody** (route-then-store ingestion). Lens A ≠ Lens B: A = products we replace; B = concepts we borrow. (Canonical tables + full index: thesis §3.5 + `comparator_analogy_registry.md`.)

The invariant: **governed contextual coherence across actors, surfaces, organizations, and authority boundaries.**

## What OMNI is not

OMNI is not a telehealth app, intake engine, scheduler, EHR, CRM, marketplace, workflow engine, AI chatbot, medspa platform, or provider network. Those are surfaces, runtimes, or wedges — projections of the substrate, not the substrate itself.

**The product OMNI ships will INCLUDE intake forms, scheduling UIs, charts, inboxes, provider workflows, and medspa operations.** Don't conflate the substrate with those. But don't cite "OMNI is not a telehealth app" to mean "don't build intake forms." Build the artifacts; the substrate produces them precisely.

2. **One visible care team across islands** (v1 NEW — patient sees real treating provider + in-person provider + care coordinator across brands; not Hims back room).
3. Cross-slice clinical awareness as default, not feature (peptide+procedure conflict, Accutane+weight-loss interaction, duplicate HRT across providers, etc.).

6. Conversational intake as wedge feature, not standalone product.

## Case-centered care (v1 NEW — substrate capability, NOT product surface)

Medicine isn't just care lanes. It's also **cases / problems** where multiple providers converge around shared evidence to make a decision.

Your brother's cancer comment surfaced this: "I want a whole team of specialists looking at it." Today: patient drags evidence office to office, waits months, gets disconnected opinions, some "main" doctor socially becomes the plan owner. Insane but normal.

OMNI's substrate names this care mode explicitly so future product variants (oncology case coordination at year 5; cross-specialty multi-consult at year 3-5; GP-vs-gyn-vs-endo HRT co-management at v1+ wedge expansion) don't require re-architecting.

**The case-deliberation primitives** (v1 §12.6):
- `case_question` — what's being deliberated
- `evidence_bundle` — shared artifact set; one source of truth
- `consult_opinion` — bounded HUMAN opinion (never AI; consulting_provider only)
- `dissent_state` — disagreement preserved, not smoothed away
- `consensus_state` — when consensus exists, named; when not, dissent preserved
- `recommendation_packet` — final convergent recommendation OR explicit "no consensus" with options
- `decision_owner` — HUMAN; commits the plan

**Six ownership roles** (v1 §7.2) — all HUMAN-only:

| Role | What they do |
|---|---|
| `treating_owner` | Commits the plan |
| `consulting_provider` | Bounded opinion only; does NOT commit |
| `ordering_provider` | Places orders if authorized |
| `supervising_provider` | Reviews / approves (e.g., NP under MD) |
| `monitoring_owner` | Owns follow-up loop |
| `open_loop_owner` | Owns next unresolved step |

**Patient is ultimate authority** over who treats them. They choose treating_owner (passively via active care relationship, or actively via patient app). They can transfer care or dismiss treating_owner. The system makes the current ownership state explicit; the patient is informed, not socially manipulated.

**Day 1 product stays narrow.** The substrate ships full primitive set; day-1 product uses it minimally (duplicate-HRT-prescribing guardrail; visible care team UI). Year 5+ product variants use the full set.

**Anti-RealSelf rule (v1 §13.6)**:

> *"Not second opinions. Shared evidence, bounded roles, explicit ownership."*

OMNI doesn't ship an opinion marketplace. Ever.

## Per-event ownership orthogonality (v2 NEW §7.5.1)

Every event-bearing substrate object (care_commitment, clinical_adoption, commerce_order, artifact records, messaging events) carries **7 orthogonal ownership dimensions**:

```
surface_of_record           which UI/app initiated the action
channel_of_record           which medium (voice / SMS / app / phone / in-person / async portal)
operator_of_record          who operates/fulfills the service
clinical_owner              who owns clinical accountability (HUMAN-only per §7.2)
commerce_owner              who owns payment/economics
artifact_custodian          who holds the artifact + visibility scope
care_coordination_owner     who owns cross-context coordination
```

> *"Surface, channel, operator, clinical, commerce, custody, coordination — seven independent ownership dimensions. None collapses into another."*

Worked example: patient orders T panel via OMNI Direct (surface=OMNI Direct) → at OMNI Labs (operator=OMNI Labs) → ordered by NAKED Dr. X (clinical_owner=NAKED Dr. X) → billed against NAKED insurance (commerce_owner=NAKED) → custody at {patient, OMNI Labs, NAKED Dr. X under shared_context_grant} → coordinated by OMNI Patient CNS. Seven different actors on one care_commitment.

**Primitive sprawl criterion** (locked discipline):

> *"Any dimension whose conflation with another would create regulatory, strategic, data-ownership, accountability, or audit ambiguity is a separate primitive. Otherwise, collapse it."*

The 7 dimensions pass the test. Future additions presumed unnecessary unless they meet the criterion.

## Cross-operator coordination doctrine (v2 NEW §7.8)

> *"Cross-operator coordination defaults to passive coherence (Patient CNS observe + signal). Active coordination (loop closure, conflict reconciliation, cross-domain follow-ups) requires an explicit `care_coordination_owner`."*

**Default mode — Passive coherence**: care_coordination_owner = OMNI Patient CNS. Observes ALL patient-related substrate events across operators. Signals cross-operator insights (gaps, conflicts, missing follow-ups, duplicate-therapy attempts per §12.7). Does NOT close loops; alerts operator-level CNS to act.

**Elective mode — Active coordination**: care_coordination_owner = explicit operator. Possible active coordinators: OMNI Navigation (Core Capability operator) / brand care coordinator / partner coordinator / patient-self. Active coordinator owns: closing loops, reconciling conflicts, scheduling cross-domain follow-ups.

## CNS scope architecture — master line (v2 NEW §7.6)

> *"Workstreams have operators. Patients have coherence. Networks have governance."*

CNS is SCOPED to responsibility, not omniscient:

- **Operator CNS** executes within scope (delivers care; commits actions; owns workstream)
- **OMNI Patient CNS** observes + signals (never operates care; surfaces cross-context insights)
- **Network Governance Plane** observes + enforces policy (never operates care; surfaces audit events)

Not 4 CNSs governing the patient. 3 operator workstreams + 1 patient coherence layer + 1 governance layer = manageable architecture.

**CNS depth varies by operator type** (v2 clarification): a commerce-only operator (OMNI Store) has a lightweight operational runtime (catalog + fulfillment + commerce_order + audit). A clinical Specialty Line (OMNI HRT, OMNI Nephrology) has full clinical CNS depth (care_commitments + providers + protocols + clinical_adoption + monitoring + follow-up + duplicate-therapy guardrail). Diagnostic operators (OMNI Labs) sit between. The word CNS names scoped coordination responsibility — not equal clinical depth across operator types.

## AI as bounded participant — substrate-vs-care boundary (v1 SHARPENED §9 + NEW §12.8)

OMNI is NOT "AI everything." OMNI is **substrate-with-AI-as-bounded-participant**.

**Knox's refinement** (the doctrine line that matters):

> *"AI models are replaceable substrate participants. AI outputs are provenance-bearing artifacts/candidates. AI does not hold care relationships, own cases, issue consult opinions as clinicians, or commit plans."*

And:

> *"Model-pluggable substrate, not model-choice medicine."*

**Substrate side** (AI participates here): model_output_artifact, extracted_assertion (CANDIDATE; needs human validation), risk_classification (signal; queue priority hint), summary (provenance-bearing; not truth), draft_message (draft; not sent), disagreement_signal (alert when 2+ models extract divergently). All non-authoritative.

**Care side** (AI NEVER authors here): consult_opinion, committed_plan, authorized_action, order, prescription, final_recommendation. All HUMAN-only per §7.2 ownership hierarchy.

**The boundary is structurally enforced**, not just policy-prohibited. RLS + capability checks + type system + audit lineage all prevent AI from authoring care-side artifacts.

### AI capability + model registry (v1 NEW §12.8)

OMNI is model-pluggable through governed registry:
- `ai_capability` — named bounded task category (extract / summarize / classify / draft / compare / coding / routing / risk-classify)
- `ai_model_registry` — approved models per capability

## The universal flow — two interlocking governed loops

OMNI runs **two interlocking governed loops, bridged by authority gates** (not one linear chain; the chain below is the unrolled view):
- **Sense loop (information):** source_event → observation → extracted_assertion → review → committed assertion → context. *What is true, by whom, at what authority.* (Tesla-signal analogy.)
- **Act loop (fulfillment):** request → authorize → order → fulfill → output → review/release → follow-up. *What was requested, and did it get done.* Its primitives — `fulfillment_order` (lab/Rx/commerce/kit/device specialize it) and `care_obligation` (due/recall/retest/refill) — are co-equal to the sense loop's observation/assertion. (Amazon-fulfillment analogy.)
- **Authority gates between them** (Houston go/no-go): verification, clinical adoption, eligibility/consent, payment. A signal doesn't become an act, and an act output doesn't become truth, without the gate.
- **Interlock:** an act's output (lab result, Rx dispense) re-enters the sense loop; a sense result or due obligation triggers an act. CNS coordinates the cycle; owns neither loop's truth.

**Payload-noun ≠ domain:** labs/Rx/commerce/messages/appointments/wearables are use-cases threading BOTH loops + several domains — never one domain. A lab = an act (order/fulfill) producing sense (value→Observation, report→D7, finding→Clinical Memory). Decompose before naming.

Every input modality enters the sense loop through one chain. Every act exits through one fulfillment chain. The CNS is invariant; modalities are interchangeable at the boundary.

```
INPUT (form / lab / wearable / voice / live AI conversation /
       device / external Rx / message / appointment / self-report /
       external chart / robot telemetry)
  → source_event
  → media_artifact
  → observation
  → extraction_run (AI / rule / policy; model_version + capability_envelope pinned per §12.8)
  → extracted_assertion (CANDIDATE; with confidence, uncertainty, contradiction_flag)
  → patient_confirmation / clinician_review  ← HUMAN here per §12.8 substrate-vs-care boundary
  → committed assertion
  → CNS context_packet update
  → candidate (prescribe / message / schedule / escalate / coordinate / etc.)
  → policy_resolver (deterministic rules + policy version pinned)
  → authorized_action (actor authority verified, scope checked, audited; HUMAN-committed)
  → execution
  → OUTPUT (SMS / Rx / appointment / lab order / robot session /
            chart materialization / payment / provider alert /
            patient app update / external chart export)
  → evidence_record (audit, delivery, outcome lineage)
  → feedback into next source_event
```

Adding a new input modality = "what adapter produces source_events?" Not a CNS rewrite. Adding a new output modality = "what executes the authorized_action?" Not a CNS rewrite. **The CNS doesn't care what modality the input is. It cares about the chain.**

**AI participates at extraction (candidates only); humans commit.** Substrate-vs-care boundary holds throughout the flow.

## Provider choice, ownership, boundaries, custody, governance (v1 completion pass 2026-05-25)

After v1 spawned, a same-day pressure-test surfaced 4 more substrate gaps. v1 absorbed them in-place via completion pass (per Operating Contract §17). New sections in §7 family:

### §7.3 The 4-level framework

Every care interaction in OMNI runs through 4 levels:

1. **Preference** — who the patient wants / allows / excludes
2. **Assignment** — who is booked / performs
3. **Care commitment** — what was actually committed (procedure / Rx / plan / recommendation / order / monitoring obligation)
4. **Scoped ownership** — who owns it at appropriate scope (episode / series-or-plan / therapy-lane / case / deliberation)

The threshold rule: *"Not every event is a care commitment. A care commitment begins when accountability attaches."*

Maps cleanly: wearable signals = observations; retail purchases = commerce; lab kit requests = source events. They become care_commitments only when accountability attaches — when a provider/protocol commits to ordering / interpreting / monitoring / acting on them.

**Founding longitudinal-signal case (Alec Harris, §7.3):** a real peptide patient reported off-the-chart wearable sleep scores, muscle gain, energy, and a resolved tennis elbow — *while asking if his dose was right* — in a plain text thread the provider had to catch manually. That is the lived future OMNI is built for: turn longitudinal context (wearable + patient-reported + dose/refill/lab) into a **governed care opportunity** (dose review / labs / follow-up / care_commitment) — never raw signal as clinical truth. **The future is OMNI helping providers help patients, not providers manually catching every pattern every time.** (Provenance: `evidence/omni_field_cases.md` FIELD-001; solve-obligation: `D0THES-REV-142`.)

Aesthetic medicine is NOT outside this. A Botox visit has episode-scope ownership (provider owns the episode + complication loop). A Sculptra series has series-or-plan scope ownership. HRT has therapy-lane scope ownership. Oncology has case scope ownership. **Same substrate; different scope; always an owner.**

Lifecycle events on commitments (not separate primitives): assigned / accepted / activated / revised / scope_changed / transferred / shared / completed / cancelled / expired / reopened.

Ambiguity is handled too: when ownership is unclear (knee revision unclear follow-up; 2 ortho providers same knee), OMNI creates an ambiguity-resolution commitment with coordination_owner queue. Even ambiguity has scoped accountability. Multi-provider care is fine if scopes are explicit; chaos appears when scopes overlap silently.

### §7.4 Boundary modes and the 7 concepts that aren't the same

Brand ≠ location ≠ group ≠ legal entity ≠ tenant ≠ provider ≠ care team. May align early. Will diverge at scale. Substrate must not hardcode them as equivalent.

7 boundary modes operate as runtime policy envelopes around any commitment:
within_brand / within_owned_group / cross_brand_same_omni / cross_legal_entity_same_omni / external_partner_network / external_nonpartner_evidence_only / emergency_break_glass.

Same substrate. Different policy envelope per boundary.

**Care team is patient-scoped graph, not org chart.** Patient may have HRT team (Dr A + Dr B linked) + aesthetic team (Dr C isolated) — same patient, one OMNI account, multiple per-scope care teams. Patient toggles sharing per provider per scope.

Patient partitioning is allowed. Hidden partitioning is not. Restricted sharing creates degraded safety state that must be surfaced, not hidden. Provider may decline or limit care if required context is unavailable.

### §7.5 Artifact custody, portability, and clinical adoption

8 distinct object types that must NOT collapse: source_artifact / custody / visibility_grant / clinical_adoption (NEW primitive) / derived_assertion / provider_note / longitudinal_projection / operational_commerce_record.

The key insight: **sharing an artifact is not the same as adopting an interpretation. Patient-portable does not mean authority-portable.**

MRI travels from Oncology 1 to Oncology 2 as source_artifact (under shared_context_grant). Oncology 1's interpretation does NOT auto-transfer. Oncology 2 independently makes a `clinical_adoption` decision (accept prior / reject / reinterpret / view-only).

Provider notes / receipts / brand consents / internal pricing don't auto-share cross-network. Active meds + allergies + labs + upcoming visits do (per grant + scope). Specific category contents defer to Phase G + product/legal.

Brand-contract implication: Powered-by-OMNI brands do NOT buy "all uploaded data forever." Patient-portable core context stays patient-directed. OMNI does NOT claim unilateral right to expose brand-authored clinical judgment outside grants.

### §7.6 The OMNI Network Governance Plane

At 10-year scale, OMNI has three levels:

1. Patient care substrate
2. Boundary policy envelope
3. Network governance plane

The governance plane exists above tenants/brands. It governs AI model registry + templates + rules + safety surveillance + audit + support access + abuse/fraud monitoring + brand configuration + permission policy. But it is NOT a blanket chart-viewer.

**OMNI may govern the network without owning every clinical judgment inside the network. Network oversight is not blanket chart access.**

Aggregate-by-default. Patient-identifiable access ONLY by named purpose (support request / safety incident / audit / break-glass / explicit grant / contractual oversight). Minimum-necessary. Policy-scoped. Logged. Reviewable.

Differentiator from Henry Ford: institutional clinical OS (Henry Ford owns chart) vs networked clinical OS (OMNI governs substrate without owning every interpretation). OMNI doesn't become Henry Ford by being aggregate-by-default + boundary-aware + anti-superuser + anti-data-grab.
